What it shows

The control

Before reading anything into the curve: on easy ordinary missions, where a one-body model is accurate (η around 0.005), both routes arrive 100% of the time. That tie is the control. It had to be earned — at 12 control segments the approximate route scored 83% against the idealised route's 83%, and at 24 segments 83% against 100%, not because the coarse model was wrong but because the search was too coarse to fly either plan well. Only at 48 segments (about 3 s each) do the two tie where theory says they must. Any gap below is therefore a gap between the models, not between search budgets.

What the curve is made of

Rungs are set by D = η / A: how wrong a one-body model is where the ship has to go, divided by how easily the tank can absorb being wrong. η comes from waypoint placement, A from the fuel and engine. Both are closed-form at sampling time, so each rung was solved for rather than found by trial — the expensive planning below ran only on the levels already known to sit where they were wanted.

MDL is the fewest piecewise-constant segments that still arrive, found by coarsening the idealised plan rather than re-searching it: control description length in a fixed basis. Switches counts changes in the plan after snapping it to the nine controls a person actually has. A level whose answer is one long burn is not interesting however hard it is, and one that arrives with the engine off is a free ride.

Demand on its own does not make a level worth playing, and neither does a busy control strip. The two together separate the cases that matter — and the easy one to miss is the bottom-left corner, a single decisive burn that scores as dull on action count alone:
